DESCRIPTION

i.delineate.opencl delineates agricultural fields in a 3-band imagery group with Delineate-Anything, a resolution-agnostic field boundary model (Lavreniuk et al. 2025), and writes them as a vector area map. The network, an Ultralytics YOLO11 instance segmentation model, runs on an OpenCL device (GPU or CPU) with its weights read directly from the published PyTorch file: neither Python nor PyTorch is needed at run time. The detected field masks are then composed, merged across tiles and filtered as the reference implementation's main method does, and vectorized into a topologically clean map.

The group (or its subgroup) must contain exactly three raster maps, used as the red, green and blue channels in the order they are listed in the group (see i.group). Integer maps within 0-255 are used as they are; other maps are stretched linearly between the lower and upper percentile of their positive values to the 0-255 range the model was trained on. Cells that are NULL in any band, or 0 in all three (black, the reference's no-data value), hold no field. Processing is done in the current computational region.

Weights

The three models published by the authors are supported; the network is built from the model description stored in the file, so the model is recognised by its content:

model File Network Size
large_v2 (best quality) DelineateAnythingv2.pt YOLO11x-seg 125 MB
large DelineateAnything.pt YOLO11x-seg 125 MB
small (fastest) DelineateAnything-S.pt YOLO11n-seg 18 MB

Without a checkpoint file, the module looks for the published file names in the weights folder: weights_dir, by default $DELINEATE_MODELS_DIR, or ~/models/delineate when that environment variable is not set. The best model present is used (large_v2, then large, then small), or the one chosen with model. A missing model is downloaded into that folder on first use (large_v2 when the folder holds none); the download is checked against the published size, resumes where an interrupted one stopped, and waits for another run downloading the same file. The -n flag forbids downloading.

The pickled model is read without running any of its code: classes are never imported, objects only become records of their class name and attributes.

Delineation

The model sees 512 x 512 pixel tiles. Imagery with cells of 4 m or more is upsampled twice with OpenCV's bicubic kernel first (scale=auto; 1, 2 or 4 can be forced), so that a tile covers 256 x 256 region cells of 10 m Sentinel-2 imagery; the fields are then delineated at half the region resolution. Tiles overlap: they are placed every tile_step times their size (half a tile by default) in the reference's order.

Each tile is run through the network. Detections above the confidence threshold go through Ultralytics' non-maximum suppression (IoU 0.7, at most 300) and their masks are built at full tile resolution from the mask prototypes, then opened and closed with a 3 x 3 square. Of each mask, the largest outer contour is kept, filled, and cut by the clip classes of the land cover mask. Detections smaller than min_pixels tile cells, or lying for less than valid_fraction of their area in valid data (at least 2 cells away from no data) outside the filter classes, are dropped. Overlapping detections of a tile are merged when their overlap (IoU) exceeds 0.8, the larger ones placed first. Where tiles overlap, the smaller field wins each cell, and fields of neighbouring tiles are merged into one when they overlap enough (IoU above merge_iou, or within the 4-cell edge band of a tile with an IoU above 0.6 or more than 192 cells in common, and covering enough of each other). Finally the borders between fields are cleared and the gaps filled from the neighbouring fields (Delineate-Anything's identity opening), which leaves a thin line between adjacent fields.

These steps and their defaults are those of the reference implementation (methods/main with conf_sample.yaml).

Land cover mask

An optional land cover mask (integer classes) refines the fields: cells of the clip_classes (e.g. water, forest) are cut out of the fields; a detection lying for more than 1 - valid_fraction of its area in the filter_classes (e.g. built-up land) is dropped; and cells of the background_classes where no field was found become background areas, kept when larger than background_min_area with their holes smaller than background_min_hole filled. NULL mask cells belong to no class. The mask is read at the region resolution (nearest neighbour).

Filtering and vectorization

Holes in a field smaller than min_hole square meters are filled, then fields smaller than min_area square meters are dropped. Both thresholds are given for 10 m cells and scaled by the cell area divided by 100 m² unless the -u flag is set (2500 m² for 10 m cells, 625 m² for 5 m cells). Areas are computed from the cells, on the ellipsoid in latitude-longitude projects. A small field lying in a filled hole is absorbed; a field large enough to be kept stays an island.

Boundaries are traced along cell edges into shared topological boundaries and simplified with the Douglas-Peucker algorithm with the simplify tolerance, by default twice the cell diagonal of the region (28 m for 10 m cells), the tolerance of the reference's simplification step; 0 keeps the raster staircase. Junctions between fields are kept, so neighbouring fields stay adjacent. The topology is then cleaned and each field gets one centroid, placed at the cell farthest from its border. The optional raster output holds the field categories before simplification, at the delineation resolution.

Attribute table

Column Description
cat Field category
background 1 for a background area from the land cover mask, else 0
class Land cover class of a background area, else 0
area Area before simplification, in square meters

OpenCL device

With device=auto, the first GPU is used, else the first CPU device. When several OpenCL platforms expose the same device, the first platform on which the kernels compile is used; platform restricts the choice to platforms whose name contains the given text. Mesa's rusticl only exposes a GPU when enabled in the environment, e.g. RUSTICL_ENABLE=radeonsi.

Device profile and precision

The profile adapts the run to the device:

  • cpu: a CPU device such as PoCL. The whole network runs on the host over the mapped device buffers (no copy): convolutions as BLAS matrix products split into cache-sized blocks over the OpenMP threads, the other layers as OpenMP loops. PoCL runs the GPU-shaped kernels about ten times slower. Without a BLAS library at build time the OpenCL kernels are used.
  • igpu: a GPU sharing host memory (integrated graphics, APU). The input and the network outputs live in host-visible memory and are written and read by mapping them (zero copy); the host prepares the next batch of tiles while the device runs the current one.
  • dgpu: a GPU with its own memory on internal PCI Express. Tiles are uploaded from pinned host memory without blocking, and two batches are in flight.
  • egpu: a GPU behind a slow link (Thunderbolt or USB4 enclosure, PoCL-Remote). As dgpu, with uploads and downloads on their own command queue so that they overlap the computation. The weights and activations stay on the device for the whole run.

With profile=auto, the profile is detected: cpu for a CPU device, igpu for a GPU reporting unified memory, egpu for a remote device or a GPU whose narrowest PCI Express link to the host carries less than 6 GB/s (read from the driver's PCI address and Linux sysfs), dgpu otherwise. The chosen profile and the reason are printed with --verbose. A profile can be forced to compare them; igpu needs a device sharing host memory.

precision=fp16 stores the activations as IEEE half-precision numbers, read and written with the core OpenCL vload_half and vstore_half functions (no half-precision extension is needed); the arithmetic stays in single precision. It halves the memory traffic of a bandwidth-bound GPU, and is not available with cpu. precision=auto uses fp32. fp16 moves boxes by less than 0.2 pixel and scores by about 0.005, which is enough to tip detections lying at the confidence threshold: on the Sentinel-2 sample, it gives 147 fields instead of 141, 130 of them matching the fp32 result with an intersection over union above 0.95. Delineate-Anything itself runs in half precision on GPUs, with the same effect.

Memory

The fields are delineated over the whole region at once: the module needs about 12 bytes per output cell (an identity and a weight per cell, then the vectorization), plus 9 bytes per region cell for the imagery and masks. A Sentinel-2 tile of 10980 x 10980 cells, delineated at 5 m, needs about 6 GiB. Split larger areas with g.region. On the device, the large models need about 1.2 GiB of activations for the default batch of 4 tiles in fp32.

Debugging aids

I_DELINEATE_OPENCL_PROFILE=1 prints, per kernel (or host operation), the calls, the time, and the achieved GFLOP/s and GB/s, which shows whether it is limited by compute or by memory bandwidth; each kernel is timed alone with the wall clock, which also works where driver event times are wrong. When I_DELINEATE_OPENCL_DUMP names a directory, the input and the output of every layer of the first tile, and every detection, are written there for comparison with the PyTorch reference. I_DELINEATE_OPENCL_DOWNLOAD_URL replaces the download location (https://huggingface.co/MykolaL/DelineateAnything/resolve/main), for a local mirror.

NOTES

The port reproduces the reference results: on the Sentinel-2 sample shipped with Delineate-Anything (1572 x 1540 cells at 10 m, small model), it finds the same 141 fields, every one matching its reference polygon with an intersection over union above 0.9 (median 0.9998) before simplification. With the large_v2 model on a 768 x 768 cell crop, all 111 fields match (intersection over union above 0.95, median 1.0). The network outputs match PyTorch to single precision (relative error below 3e-4).

On a laptop Core i7-1165G7 (PoCL, cpu profile with OpenBLAS), the whole sample takes 26 s with the small model, against about 4 minutes for the reference implementation; the 768 x 768 crop takes 100 s with large_v2, against 5 minutes 14 s. On the same laptop's Intel Iris Xe GPU (igpu profile), the sample takes 46 s of delineation: the GEMM kernel reaches only about 10 GFLOP/s there, so device=cpu is the faster choice on such machines (I_DELINEATE_OPENCL_PROFILE=1 shows the rate reached on a given device).

Differences from the reference implementation:

  • The region is processed as one block, so the reference's merging of polygons cut by its processing regions and worker blocks is not needed. Small holes are filled and fields filtered on the raster, in a topological map where polygons cannot overlap.
  • The network runs in fp32 by default, on GPUs as well (the reference uses fp16 on GPUs).
  • Merging tiles is done in the reference's tile order on one thread, so the result does not depend on the scheduling of parallel workers.
  • The additional background vector source and multi-model passes of the reference configuration are not supported.

EXAMPLES

Sentinel-2 true colour image

Delineate fields in a Sentinel-2 RGB composite at 10 m (the region is upsampled twice, fields delineated at 5 m):

i.group group=s2_rgb input=B04,B03,B02
g.region raster=B04 -p
i.delineate.opencl group=s2_rgb output=fields
v.db.univar fields column=area

Land cover mask

With a land cover map whose classes 1 and 2 are water and forest, 3 is built-up land and 4 grassland, cut water and forest out of the fields, drop fields in built-up areas and add grassland where no field is found:

i.delineate.opencl group=s2_rgb output=fields mask=landcover \
    clip_classes=1,2 filter_classes=3 background_classes=4

Fast preview on the CPU

i.delineate.opencl group=s2_rgb output=fields_quick model=small \
    device=cpu --verbose
